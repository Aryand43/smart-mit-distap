The method

How the best model works, in five steps

1

Normalise

Divide each image by that day’s healthy-plant average, so only stress differences remain

2

Cut

Cut the plant into about 9,000 overlapping patches

3

Describe

A pretrained ResNet-50 turns each patch into numbers

4

Label

A small classifier names a condition for each patch

5

Vote

The most common label among the patches is the plant’s answer

Patch and stride

Patch: a 224 × 224 pixel square of the 608 × 968 image. Stride: each patch starts 4 pixels from the last

Fair test

Train on two plants, test on the third it has never seen; each plant takes a turn

Repeated runs

15 runs = 3 test plants × 5 trainings from different random starts. Each training lasts 60 epochs

Being tuned

These settings were fixed by hand. Bayesian optimisation is now tuning patch size, stride, epochs and learning rate

An epoch is one pass of training over all training patches; a run is one complete training from a fresh random start. PLS-DA is only a comparison method (results table), not part of this model: its 60 runs are 3 test plants × 20 different random sets of 5,000 pixels per training image. A patch is 224 by 224 pixels, the input size ResNet-50 expects. It is not 9,000 pixels: 9,000 is the number of patches per plant. Only patches that are at least 40% plant are kept, so background is ignored. Training uses patches 56 pixels apart, with flips and rotations on training only. ResNet-50 is pretrained on ImageNet and kept frozen; a small softmax head is trained with cross-entropy loss and weight decay. The healthy-plant average comes from the two training plants only, so nothing about the test plant leaks in.
